# Transition engine: a finite region between a stationary past and a stationary continuation

## Domain and grid

Keep the finite engine's `(t, age)` coordinates and extend the domain to the strip `0 <= t <= T, 0 <= a <= L`, with `L` the lag window of the past and of the continuation (`horizon.lag_window`). Below the diagonal `a <= t` are the new shocks (`s = t - a >= 0`), exactly today's triangle; above it, `t < a <= L`, are the old shocks (`s = -c`, pre-age `c = a - t` in `(0, L]`), which exist only while `t < L`. Shocks are dropped past age `L` for old and new alike, so at every `t >= L` a time row is the stationary engine's age axis: that is what makes the stationary solution an exact fixed point (test 2) and what the closure at `T` relies on. One breakpoint sequence serves both axes, cut at `T` in time and at `L` in age: for models with lags the multiples of the unit (old kernels kink at ages `k tau`, delayed effects of the switch at times `k tau`; both axes lag-aligned so lagged reads and delayed-row maps stay node-to-node `map_shift`s, piece `(p, q) -> (p - k, q - k)`); for models without lags the default is multiples of `L` (Kyle-Back: `{0, 8, 16}`), the old kernel being read by spectral 1-D interpolation, and `horizon.breakpoints` refines. Pieces: `(p, q)` with `q < p` rectangles and `q = p` lower Duffy triangles (unchanged, same offsets, same order, listed first within each time panel), then the old pieces of panel `p`: the upper Duffy triangle of square `(p, p)` (`a = t + theta (t_{p+1} - t)`, degenerate at the far corner) and rectangles `(p, q)`, `q > p`. `interp`, `contains`, `row_weights(t)` (now over `[0, L]`), `mass`, `mass_matrix(rho)` and `_crossings` (adding the crossing of `a = t`) extend to the new kinds; a grid without a past is bitwise today's grid. Discrete initial shocks (`s = 0-`, point masses) live on the time grid (the panels' Lobatto nodes in `t`), like the impulse columns but with a point observation.

## Unknowns and layout

Primary kernels `Z` on `N = N_new + N_band` nodes, one column per Brownian channel (a shock time in `[-L, T]` is one function on the strip), then one column per initial shock (1-D in `t`), then impulse columns. Raw maps `g[u][r](t', b)` on the same strip stored at the shifted time as now; band nodes `b > t'` are the weights on pre-zero increments (`u = t' - b < 0`), and each initial shock adds a discrete weight `g_init[u][r][i](t)` per row. `_identified` keeps a band node iff its raw increment is inside the window (`b + d <= L`, the stationary rule) and the old row carries something there; unobserved initial shocks and zero pasts are masked, so their unknowns never enter a solve. The closed loop of old and new shocks is decoupled (a kernel's Volterra problem is per shock time): the new region is solved by the current `_solve_causal` on the current arrays; the band and the discrete columns by their own causal solves. Every operator is assembled as (new part, today's code) + (band part) + (discrete part); with a zero past the band part is skipped, which is what makes test 1 bit-for-bit rather than to round-off (BLAS blocking changes bits when dimensions change).

## Operators that change

The rule: a line integral or read that leaves `[0, T]` on the left reads the old kernel, one that leaves it on the right reads the new stationary kernel; both are one-dimensional functions of age and enter as affine terms.

* State part (`_state_part`): band nodes get `e^{A t} X_old(c)` (the old closed-loop state kernel at age `c` already contains every pre-zero input), the Volterra path from `r = 0` (not `s`) to `t`, and a lagged input `name@tau` at `t < tau` read as `K_old(name, t - tau + c)`, a known vector folded into `B0`. Initial columns: `e^{A t} loads`.
* Reads: `read(dt, da)` on a band node whose read time is negative returns zero and a `past_read` supplies the old value; `map_shift` shifts `(p, q) -> (p - k, q - k)` for band pieces too. Delayed rows below their delay read the old row kernel `y_old(t - d + c)` (`_seen_rows` gains a known addend).
* `conv_left(g, d)`: the pre-zero segment `int_{s+d}^{0} g(t, t - v) y_old(v - s) dv` couples the band map (unknown, iterate) to the old row: a `PastPath` whose `I` reads band map nodes and whose `J` is the old `AgeGrid.interp` at age `v - s`; it depends on the maps, so it enters `B` each `closed_loop` call. Initial columns: `g_init(t)` times the row's jump plus the convolution over `[0, t]`.
* `response_op` / impulse responses: unchanged (impulses arrive at `r >= 0`, deviations before 0 are sunk; `R` is zero on the band).
* `continuation_op`: `[t, T]` on the strip as now, plus the tail (below).
* `projection_op`: for map nodes with `u >= 0` the shock range becomes `[-L, u]` (band `phi` against band `y`); for band map nodes (`u < 0`) `y_raw(u, s) = y_old(u - s)` is a `PastPath`; the discrete weights get a scalar regression per time node, `E[phi_t xi_i]`, on the observed combinations of initial shocks (collinear point observations are merged, never left to a singular Gram).
* `cost_mass`, `expected_cost`, the loss form and the second-order form: over the strip plus a time-quadrature sum for discrete columns; costs stay additive over shocks.

## FOC and the stationary continuation at T

The per-date condition is unchanged in form; `Fu` on band nodes has the instantaneous term, the continuation over `[t, min(T, s + L)]` and the own-lag read. With `closure: stationary` (the default for kind `transition`) the engine solves the new model's stationary equilibrium once on window `L` (`self.limit`, a `StationaryResult`, cached, `check()`ed) and every integral or read that runs past `T` is evaluated on it: for a new shock `s > T - L`, `TAIL_j(t, s) = int_T^{s+L} e^{-rho(tau - t)} r_stat,j(tau - t) (Q zeta)_stat,j(tau - s) dtau` (the stationary engine's own integrand), and the own-lag read of flows in `(T, T + tau]` likewise. The tail is `gamma`-independent (the world beyond `T` is the stationary world), so it enters `bvec` only; that is exact when the equilibrium is stationary from `T - L` on, which is what the `settled` guard checks, and at a stationary fixed point the stationary maps solve the approximate system exactly (both residuals vanish there), which is test 2. Old shocks (`s + L <= L <= T`) never need a tail. `closure: end` keeps today's terminal behaviour (the canonical finite-horizon Kyle-Back with a prior, where `T` matters and the new model's stationary game is degenerate). The exact alternative, a buffer `[T, T + L]` on which the world is solved under the frozen stationary maps, is kept as the fallback if the guard proves too restrictive.

## The past object and API

`noisestate/past.py`: `Past(window, grid: AgeGrid, kernels by primary and channel, rows by agent, maps, initial: [InitialShock(name, loads {state: coef}, rows {"agent.row": coef})], model, params, converged, residual, means)` with `read(name, channel, ages, side)`, `row_read(agent, r, channel, ages, side)`, `from_result(res, window)`, `from_model(model, **solve_kw)` (solves stationary, `check()`s), `from_spec(spec, new_model)`, `to_dict()` (the spec, not the kernels: the payload rebuilds it by re-solving). Ways to give it: `solve(model, past=StationaryResult | Model | dict)`, a file block

```yaml
horizon: {kind: transition, window: 20.0, lag_window: 10.0, nodes: 12, closure: stationary}
past: {params: {p2: 3.0}, horizon: {nodes: 24}}           # this model at p2 = 3, solved stationary on the fly
past: {model: old.yaml, params: {...}}                     # a different old model
past: {initial: [{name: V0, loads: {V: sigma0}, rows: {trader1.y1: sigma0}}]}   # a prior; closure: end
```

and `transition(old, new, T, nodes=..., lag_window=None, closure="stationary", limit=None, **solve_kw)` which attaches `res.past` and `res.limit`. `TransitionSolver(SpectralFiniteSolver)` takes `past`, `limit` and `closure` as constructor options (so `solve()`'s routing, `refine()` and `stability()` work unchanged); `ENGINES["transition"]`; kind `finite` rejects a past. Validation (all `ValueError`s naming the item): channels, states, agents, their controls and row names must match by name; coefficients, precisions, losses, discount, `myopic`, ties and naive observers may differ; delays may differ only if every old lag is a multiple of the transition's unit (message names the unit to set); the old horizon must be stationary; the past's window must cover `lag_window`; a `Model` past must converge (`ConvergenceError` otherwise), a `StationaryResult` past is accepted as given and its `converged` flag becomes a diagnostic row; with ties the past must be tie-symmetric; `loads` are states, `rows` existing rows; `closure: stationary` needs `T >= lag_window`. A known `x0` and the old regime's constant means do not touch the variance solve (LQG separation): `x0` is an initial column observed by every agent and `past.means` (states, controls, row drifts) are the mean layer's initial condition and its pre-zero lagged reads; the band maps and discrete weights are what carry inherited beliefs into the mean paths, so the mean solver on `means` consumes `res.maps` on the strip, `res.past.means` and the time-grid machinery of the discrete columns.

## Results

`TransitionResult(TriangleResult)`, kind `transition`: `grid.s` negative on the band, `evaluate(name, ch, t, s)` for `s` in `[-L, T]`, `kernel(name, "V0")` on `res.times` for initial shocks (`res.shocks` = channels + initial names), `flow_cost(agent) -> (times, E[loss(t)])`, `belief_error(agent, name) -> (times, Var[name(t) - E[name(t) | agent]])` by the per-time projection on the agent's raw rows (the market maker's `V` gives the pricing-error variance), `costs` (the discounted integral over `[0, T]`, bit-identical to the finite engine), `limit_costs` (the new stationary flows), `excess_costs` (`int_0^T e^{-rho t} (flow - limit) dt`, finite at `rho = 0`), `past`, `limit`, `settled` (largest relative deviation of the last time rows' kernels and flows from the limit over `[T - L, T]`; `SETTLED_TOL = 0.02`), `extend(window)` (re-solve on a longer `T` started from this result plus the limit beyond it, reporting the change like `refine()`), `start="limit"`. `diagnose()` rows: `past`, `past_window` (the old tail), `limit`, `settled` (flag `TRANSITION NOT SETTLED by T - L: raise horizon.window`). `to_dict()` adds `past` (spec, residual, converged, initial), `closure`, `lag_window`, `region` and `s` per node, `times`, `flow_costs`, `limit_costs`, `excess_costs`, `settled`; `map_convention` says nodes with `s < 0` weigh pre-zero increments. Plots: kernels by shock time with `s < 0` dashed; the flow-cost transient with the old and new levels and the `T - L` line; belief-error paths. `sweep(model, param, values, past=...)` keeps one past across a slider over the change size, warm-starting on the union kernels; CLI: `noisestate solve transition.yaml [--past old.yaml] [--closure end]`.

## Tests, ordered by what they pin

1. Bit identity: kind `transition`, no past, `closure: end` equals kind `finite` (`np.array_equal` on `Z`, maps, costs, `foc`) on `ch1_two_player_finite`, `ch1_delayed_finite` and the discounted one-agent model; a zero `StationaryResult` past with `lag_window >= T` gives the same bits (band built, masked); a past scaled by `1e-6` moves the result by `O(1e-6)`.
2. Same-model stationary past, `closure: stationary`: Ch3 at `L = 10`, the delayed one-agent model at `L = 10`, Kyle-Back `rho = 0.5`: with the stationary kernels as `init`, one evaluation has residual below `1e-9`; the solve returns `K_stat(t - s)` on the whole strip (band included) and `g_stat(b)` at every `t` to `1e-8`, constant flow costs, `settled < 1e-8`; the same past under `closure: end` deviates near `T` by more than `1e-3` (the control).
3. Exact one-agent transition (closed form, the style of `test_finite_discount`/`test_exact_delay`): a precision change `h_old -> h_new` (and `a`, `r`) at `t = 0`, `rho` in `{0, 0.5}`, `L = 12`, `T = 24`: `D = -K_new xhat`, the Kalman variance from `P_old` under the new Riccati ODE, old shocks started from the old stationary `(X, xhat)` kernels; cost to `1e-7`, kernels of old and new shocks to `1e-4`, `flow_cost` and `belief_error("a", "X") = P(t)` to `1e-7`; then the delayed variant by the method of steps.
4. Prior start: the same closed form with `X(0) ~ N(0, sigma0^2)` (`P(0) = sigma0^2`), unobserved and observed (`rows: {a.y: sigma0}`), pins the discrete columns exactly. Kyle-Back with a prior (no reference exists in `tests/refs` or `~/dissertation/numerics/kyleback`, whose runs are all stationary; the CARA terminal-settlement variant was scrapped): `Sigma(0) = sigma0^2` and `Sigma' = -lambda(t)^2 sigma_Z^2` with `lambda(t)` the price's `wZ` loading at age 0; gross profit flow `= lambda(t) sigma_Z^2` (the breakeven identity the Ch4 numerics use); the informed start earns more than the flowing-signal start; an `eps` sweep toward Back's constant `lambda = sigma0 / sigma_Z` and linear `Sigma(t)`.
5. Regime change with checkable limits: Ch3 `p2: 3 -> 10` and back: `Var X(0+)` equals the old stationary variance to `1e-10`, the last time row and the flows equal the new stationary values to `1e-6` at `T = 2L`, belief errors run from old to new; a sweep over `p2` with one past shows no `jump`. Kyle-Back `eps: 0.2 -> 0.1`: the breakeven identity date by date.
6. Guards and API: `settled` fails at `T = 1.2 L`; every validation error; `to_dict` JSON round trip; the file `past:` block through the CLI; `extend()`; `refine()`; ties with a symmetric past.
7. Performance (recorded, generous asserts): one-agent transition (3 pieces, 12 nodes) under 5 s; Ch3 change (16 nodes) under 30 s; Kyle-Back change (`24 x 16` nodes, `N ~ 1150`) under 2 min; a delayed transition capped near 5000 nodes.

## Risks

The strip has `P_t x P_a` pieces, so delayed models (unit panels on both axes) outgrow the dense `N^2` design beyond a few thousand nodes; the tail's `gamma`-independence is exact only under the guard; old shocks dropped past age `L` differ from the closed forms' infinite memory by the old window tail, so the exact tests must choose `L` accordingly; bit identity depends on the part-wise assembly discipline; the new model's stationary equilibrium must exist (Kyle-Back at `rho = 0` does not); collinear point observations need merging; the mean layer must agree on `Past.means` and the time-grid columns.

## Key decisions
- Domain is the strip 0 <= a <= L in (t, age) with old shocks above the diagonal and every shock dropped past age L, so a time row at t >= L is the stationary engine's age axis; rejected: (t, pre-age) rectangles with anti-diagonal cuts, and an untruncated old band a <= t + L, both of which break the same-model exactness and double the pieces for delayed models.
- One breakpoint sequence for both axes (unit multiples with lags, multiples of L without) with three new piece kinds appended after the current pieces of each time panel; rejected: separate t and a breakpoint sets, whose diagonal squares are not squares and whose lag shifts are not node-to-node.
- Every operator is assembled as new part (today's code on today's arrays) plus band part plus discrete part, with the old and new closed loops solved separately since they are decoupled per shock time; rejected: one shared assembly over the union, which changes bits through BLAS blocking and loses the bit-for-bit zero-past guarantee.
- The continuation beyond T reads the new model's stationary equilibrium as a gamma-independent affine tail (exact under the settled guard, exact at a stationary fixed point); rejected: solving a buffer [T, T + L] under frozen stationary maps (exact but 1.8x the pieces), kept as the fallback.
- The past is one object (Past) built from a StationaryResult, a Model solved on the fly, or a hand-built initial-shock list, given by solve(past=...), a file past: block, or transition(old, new, T); rejected: a separate engine kind per past type.
- Horizon kind transition (window T, lag_window L, closure stationary|end) as a subclass of the finite engine, with kind finite untouched and refusing a past; rejected: a past block on kind finite, which would blur the bit-identity contract.
- Initial shocks are discrete columns on the time grid with point observations (rows: {agent.row: coef}) and per-time discrete map weights, which also gives the canonical finite-horizon Kyle-Back prior under closure end and the mean layer its x0 column; rejected: modelling a prior as a band shock density near c = 0.
- Validation matches channels, states, agents, controls and rows by name and lets every coefficient, precision, loss, discount, myopic flag, tie and naive-observer set differ, with delays allowed only when the old lags are multiples of the transition's unit; rejected: requiring identical models (kills the parameter-change use) or allowing arbitrary structural changes (an old kernel for a missing quantity is undefined).
- Costs keep today's meaning (discounted integral over [0, T], bit-identical) and the transition's economics live in flow_cost paths, limit_costs, excess_costs and belief_error paths; rejected: folding the continuation's tail into res.costs.
- Test order pins bit identity first, then the same-model stationary exactness with an end-closure control, then closed forms (one-agent regime change, prior start) before Kyle-Back identities, because no finite-horizon Kyle-Back reference exists in tests/refs or the dissertation numerics; rejected: pinning Kyle-Back against Back's eps -> 0 limit as a primary test.

## Risks
- Grid size: the strip has P_t x P_a pieces, so a delayed model with unit panels on both axes (L = 8, T = 16, u = 0.5) reaches ~19k nodes, beyond the dense N^2 design; delayed transitions are limited to short windows until the uniform-panel block-Toeplitz structure in time is exploited as the age grid does.
- The stationary tail is gamma-independent only when the equilibrium is stationary from T - L on; a slowly settling transition (small discount, weak damping) needs a long T, and the settled guard is the only protection; the exact buffer alternative costs ~1.8x the pieces.
- Old shocks dropped past age L (the stationary engine's own truncation) differ from closed-form references with infinite memory by the old window tail; the exact tests must pick L so that e^{-decay L} is below the tolerance, and a random-walk state's constant kernel makes the truncation a modelling choice, not an approximation.
- Bit-for-bit identity with a zero past depends on the part-wise assembly discipline and on the new pieces keeping today's order and offsets; one shared einsum or matmul over the union breaks it silently.
- The new model's stationary equilibrium must exist and converge for closure stationary (Kyle-Back at rho = 0 does not); the limit solve raises ConvergenceError and the transition cannot proceed.
- Collinear point observations of initial shocks (two shocks through one row) make the discrete projection singular unless merged into the observed combinations; the per-time Gram ridge alone would hide it.
- Delay changes between regimes need commensurable units; otherwise old kinks fall off the panels and exactness is lost, so they are rejected, which limits information-change experiments to precisions and loadings.
- Ties with an asymmetric past cannot use the representative's map; the past must be tie-symmetric or the model untied, and the check is by tolerance.
- The mean layer on branch means must agree on Past.means, the x0 column and the time-grid operators; designing them separately risks two representations of the same discrete column.
- Performance of the FOC system grows as (nU nR N)^3 with the band nodes included; the Kyle-Back change at 24 x 16 nodes is ~1 s per best response, acceptable, but multi-trader or delayed variants may need the coarse start and diagnostics=False to stay interactive.
